Illustrations, not real scripts

Every sheet here was written by EduAakashaa to teach. The handwriting is a typeface, so these are not scans of students' work. The questions and the marking schemes are original and written in CBSE style. They are not CBSE papers or official schemes.

What the sheets show is real in kind: the marking scheme gives marks for steps, so how you write decides how many marks your knowledge earns.

Section C3 marks
Q.24 A cell of emf 12 V and internal resistance 2 Ω is connected to a 10 Ω resistor. Calculate (a) the current in the circuit, (b) the potential difference across the terminals of the cell, (c) the power dissipated in the 10 Ω resistor.
Marking scheme used for these sheets Illustrative
Value pointMarks
(a) I = E/(R + r) and I = 1 A1
(b) V = E − Ir or V = IR, and V = 10 V1
(c) P = I²R or VI with correct values, and P = 10 W1
Needs support. Used V = IR on the whole circuit, so the internal resistance vanished. Everything after that inherits the error. The only mark came from the form of P = VI.
Good. The method is complete. One missing unit cost half a mark. A habit of writing units on every line removes this leak.
Excellent. Data, formula, substitution with units, boxed answers and a one-line check. The check is not marked, but it is how errors get caught before the examiner sees them.

Section C3 marks
Q.27 Using Gauss’s law, derive an expression for the electric field at a distance r from an infinitely long straight wire carrying a uniform linear charge density λ.
Marking scheme used for these sheets Illustrative
Value pointMarks
Coaxial cylindrical Gaussian surface drawn and labelled (r, l)1
Flux through the curved surface = E(2πrl); flux through the ends is zero as E ⊥ dA1
q = λl and E = λ/(2πε₀r), radially outward1
Needs support. Quoted the law, then jumped to a remembered result with the wrong factor. In derivations the examiner gives marks for steps, so a bare result earns almost nothing.
Good. Right structure and right answer. Half a mark each went on missing labels and on not explaining why the end faces do not contribute.
Excellent. Opens by naming the surface, uses symmetry in a sentence, treats ends and curved surface separately, then states the direction.

Section C3 marks
Q.26 18 g of glucose (molar mass 180 g mol⁻¹) is dissolved in 90 g of water at 298 K. The vapour pressure of pure water at 298 K is 23.8 mm Hg. Calculate the vapour pressure of the solution.
Marking scheme used for these sheets Illustrative
Value pointMarks
n(glucose) = 0.1 mol and n(water) = 5 mol1
Mole fraction of water = 5/5.1 = 0.981
p = p°x = 23.8 × 0.98 = 23.3 mm Hg (23.33 accepted), with unit1
Needs support. The formulas are right, but masses went into a mole-fraction formula. Writing "n = mass / molar mass" as the first line of any solutions numerical prevents this.
Good. Complete and correct. The only loss is the unit on the last line.
Excellent. Names the law first, carries units through, and verifies with the relative lowering of vapour pressure, a different route to the same value.

Section C3 marks
Q.29 Give reasons: (i) Aniline does not undergo the Friedel-Crafts reaction. (ii) The pKb of aniline is higher than that of methylamine. (iii) Ethylamine is soluble in water, but aniline is not.
Marking scheme used for these sheets Illustrative
Value pointMarks
(i) –NH₂ is basic and forms a salt with AlCl₃; N becomes positive and the ring is strongly deactivated1
(ii) Lone pair on N is delocalised into the ring, so less available; in CH₃NH₂ the +I effect increases availability1
(iii) Ethylamine hydrogen-bonds with water; the large hydrophobic C₆H₅ group in aniline outweighs this1
Needs support. Each answer repeats the question or states something false. A reason sentence should contain a mechanism word: "forms salt", "delocalised", "hydrogen bonds".
Good. Two of three reasons are almost complete. Half marks were lost where the second half of a reason was left out.
Excellent. Each part names the cause, gives the equation or effect, and states the consequence. Three lines, three marks.

The 10-minute rewrite

Turn a lost-mark answer into a full-mark answer.

Do this once a day with one answer from your last test. It is the quickest way to move from the Good sheet to the Excellent sheet.

  1. Pick the answer that lost marks.Choose a question where you knew the topic but the marks were low.
  2. Compare it with the marking scheme.Write the value points on a sticky note. Tick the ones your answer earned.
  3. Write the skeleton.Only the formula lines, the diagram labels and the final line. If the skeleton is wrong, go back to the concept.
  4. Write the full answer.Data, formula, substitution, answer with unit, one line of check.
  5. Add it to your error log.Name the cause: concept, calculation, units, labelling or time.
