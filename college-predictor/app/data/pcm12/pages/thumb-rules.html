When a question looks hard

Six steps that always get you started.

A hard question usually breaks into a diagram, a principle and a substitution. The marking scheme pays for each of those, even if the final answer goes wrong.

STEP 1

Underline what is asked

Circle the quantity and its unit. Many wrong answers solve a neighbouring question.

STEP 2

List what is given

Write every value with its unit. Convert units now, not later.

STEP 3

Draw or tabulate

A circuit, a ray diagram, a Gaussian surface, a graph, a tree for probability.

STEP 4

Name the principle

Write the law or formula in symbols before using numbers. This line earns marks by itself.

STEP 5

Substitute, one line at a time

One change per line. Carry units. Keep fractions until the last step.

STEP 6

Check size, sign and unit

Is the answer reasonable? A speed above c or a negative mass means a slip.

4 min

The 4-minute rule. If a 3 or 5 mark question has not moved in four minutes, write the diagram, the principle and the set-up, then leave a gap and move on. You keep the early marks and return with fresh eyes during review.

Chapter by chapter

The rule, the hook and the trap.

Physics≈5 marks

Electric Charges and Fields

Rules

  • Pick the Gaussian surface by symmetry: sphere for a point or shell, cylinder for a line, pillbox for a sheet.
  • Line charge E = λ/(2πε₀r). Infinite sheet E = σ/(2ε₀). Inside a uniformly charged thin shell the field is zero.
  • Far from a dipole, the axial field is twice the equatorial field, and the equatorial field points opposite to p.
Memory hook

Axial is Double.

Common trap

Using the point-charge 1/r² for a line charge. It falls as 1/r.

Physics≈5 marks

Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance

Rules

  • Capacitors in series: 1/C adds. In parallel: C adds. This is the reverse of resistors.
  • Battery connected: V stays fixed. Battery disconnected: Q stays fixed. Decide which before using any formula.
  • Energy stored U = ½CV² = Q²/2C. A dielectric of constant K multiplies C by K.
Memory hook

Battery on, V stays. Battery off, Q stays.

Common trap

After disconnecting the battery, a dielectric lowers V. It does not raise Q.

Physics≈6 marks

Current Electricity

Rules

  • Terminal voltage V = E − Ir. Power to the load is maximum when R = r.
  • Metre bridge at balance: R/S = l/(100 − l). A potentiometer draws no current at balance.
  • Kirchhoff: the junction rule is charge conservation and the loop rule is energy conservation. Fix a loop direction and keep it.
Memory hook

Colour code: B B ROY of Great Britain has a Very Good Wife (Black, Brown, Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Violet, Grey, White).

Common trap

Flipping the sign of Ir when the cell is being charged.

Physics≈6 marks

Moving Charges and Magnetism

Rules

  • Straight wire: thumb along the current, fingers curl along B. Loop: fingers along the current, thumb along B.
  • F = qv × B and r = mv/qB. The period T = 2πm/qB does not depend on speed.
  • Galvanometer to ammeter: a small shunt in parallel. To voltmeter: a large resistance in series.
Memory hook

FBI for Fleming's left hand: Force thumb, B first finger, I second finger. APSV: Ammeter Parallel, Series Voltmeter.

Common trap

Applying the force rule to a negative charge without reversing the direction.

Physics≈5 marks

Magnetism and Matter

Rules

  • Diamagnetic: weak repulsion, small negative susceptibility, independent of temperature. Paramagnetic: weak attraction, small positive susceptibility, χ ∝ 1/T (Curie). Ferromagnetic: strong attraction, large positive susceptibility.
  • Magnetic field lines form closed loops. Inside a bar magnet they run from S to N.
  • Torque on a magnetic dipole in a field: τ = mB sinθ.
Memory hook

Dia = Denied (repelled). Para = Pulled weakly. Ferro = Fiercely pulled.

Common trap

Saying field lines start at N and end at S.

Physics≈4 marks

Electromagnetic Induction

Rules

  • Lenz's law: the induced current opposes the change in flux, not the flux itself.
  • emf = −dΦ/dt. Motional emf = Blv. Energy in an inductor U = ½LI².
  • Flux increasing: the induced field opposes it. Flux decreasing: the induced field supports it.
Memory hook

Nature dislikes change.

Common trap

Writing 'opposes the flux' instead of 'opposes the change in flux'.

Physics≈2 marks

Alternating Current

Rules

  • Vrms = V₀/√2 and Irms = I₀/√2. Meters read rms values.
  • Z = √(R² + (X_L − X_C)²). At resonance X_L = X_C, Z = R, current is maximum and ω₀ = 1/√(LC).
  • Power factor cos φ = R/Z. A pure inductor or capacitor uses no average power.
  • Transformer: Vs/Vp = Ns/Np. A step-up transformer raises voltage and lowers current.
Memory hook

CIVIL: in a capacitor I leads V. In an inductor V leads I.

Common trap

Using peak values in average power formulas.

Physics≈4 marks

Electromagnetic Waves

Rules

  • Displacement current I_d = ε₀ dΦ_E/dt. It keeps Ampere's law consistent across a capacitor gap.
  • E and B are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel, in phase, with E/B = c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀).
  • In order of increasing frequency: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma.
Memory hook

Raging Martians Invaded Venus Using X-ray Guns.

Common trap

Listing the spectrum in wavelength order when the question asks for frequency order.

Physics≈10 marks

Ray Optics and Optical Instruments

Rules

  • New Cartesian convention: distances from the pole, positive along the direction of the incident light.
  • Mirror: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f. Lens: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f. Lens maker's formula: 1/f = (n − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂).
  • Total internal reflection needs light going from denser to rarer and an angle above the critical angle, sin C = 1/n.
  • Telescope in normal adjustment: M = f_o/f_e. A compound microscope has a short-focus objective.
Memory hook

Mirror plus, Lens minus.

Common trap

Forgetting that u is negative for a real object.

Physics≈4 marks

Wave Optics

Rules

  • Fringe width β = λD/d. Bright at path difference nλ, dark at (n + ½)λ.
  • Single slit: the first minimum is at a sinθ = λ. The central maximum is twice as wide as the others.
  • Interference fringes are equal in width. Diffraction fringes are not.
Memory hook

Interference is even. Diffraction has a fat middle.

Common trap

Swapping d (slit separation) with D (screen distance).

Physics≈4 marks

Dual Nature of Radiation and Matter

Rules

  • Einstein: K_max = hν − φ. Stopping potential: eV₀ = K_max.
  • Frequency sets K_max. Intensity sets the photocurrent. Below the threshold frequency there is no emission at any intensity.
  • de Broglie: λ = h/p = h/√(2mK). For an electron through V volts, λ ≈ 12.27/√V Å.
Memory hook

Frequency fixes energy. Intensity fixes count.

Common trap

Saying a brighter light gives faster photoelectrons.

Physics≈4 marks

Atoms

Rules

  • Bohr: r ∝ n², v ∝ 1/n and E_n = −13.6/n² eV.
  • Series end at n = 1 (Lyman, ultraviolet), n = 2 (Balmer, visible) and n = 3 (Paschen, infrared).
  • 1/λ = R(1/n₁² − 1/n₂²), with R = 1.097 × 10⁷ m⁻¹.
Memory hook

Lyman UV, Balmer visible, Paschen infrared: lower levels 1, 2, 3.

Common trap

Using the upper level where the question gives the lower.

Physics≈4 marks

Nuclei

Rules

  • Nuclear radius R = R₀A^(1/3), so density is the same for all nuclei.
  • 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c². Binding energy = mass defect (in u) × 931.5 MeV.
  • Binding energy per nucleon peaks near iron. Light nuclei release energy by fusion and heavy ones by fission.
  • N = N₀(½)^(t/T) and the decay constant λ = 0.693/T.
Memory hook

Light fuse, heavy split, iron is the middle.

Common trap

Mixing mass in kg and in u within one calculation.

Physics≈7 marks

Semiconductor Electronics

Rules

  • N-type: pentavalent donor (P, As, Sb), electrons are the majority carriers. P-type: trivalent acceptor (B, Al, In), holes are the majority carriers.
  • Forward bias narrows the depletion layer. Reverse bias widens it.
  • A rectifier uses the one-way conduction of a diode. Half-wave uses one diode. Full-wave uses two or four.
Memory hook

Pentavalent gives Negative carriers. Trivalent gives Positive holes.

Common trap

Drawing the diode symbol with the arrow reversed.

Chemistry7 marks

Solutions

Rules

  • Raoult's law: p = p°x. Colligative properties depend on the number of particles, not their identity.
  • With the van't Hoff factor i: ΔT_f = iK_f m and π = iCRT. i > 1 for dissociation and i < 1 for association.
  • Positive deviation: weaker A–B attraction, ΔH_mix > 0, vapour pressure above ideal. Negative deviation is the opposite.
Memory hook

Colligative = Count, not Character.

Common trap

Using masses where moles are needed.

Chemistry9 marks

Electrochemistry

Rules

  • E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode. Oxidation at the anode, reduction at the cathode.
  • Nernst at 298 K: E = E° − (0.059/n) log Q. ΔG° = −nFE°.
  • Λm = κ × 1000/M in S cm² mol⁻¹, with M in mol L⁻¹. Kohlrausch: Λ°m is the sum of the ion contributions.
  • Faraday: m = ZIt, and 1 F = 96,500 C.
Memory hook

LEO says GER: Lose Electrons is Oxidation, Gain Electrons is Reduction. AN OX, RED CAT.

Common trap

Subtracting the wrong way round. Write cathode minus anode every time.

Chemistry7 marks

Chemical Kinetics

Rules

  • Order comes from experiment, not from the balanced equation.
  • Units of k: zero order mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹, first order s⁻¹, second order L mol⁻¹ s⁻¹.
  • First order: t½ = 0.693/k, independent of concentration, and k = (2.303/t) log([R]₀/[R]). Zero order: t½ = [R]₀/2k.
  • Arrhenius: k = Ae^(−Ea/RT) and log(k₂/k₁) = (Ea/2.303R)(1/T₁ − 1/T₂).
Memory hook

First order: the half-life never changes.

Common trap

Reading the order off the coefficients of the equation.

Chemistry7 marks

The d- and f-Block Elements

Rules

  • Exceptions: Cr is 3d⁵4s¹ and Cu is 3d¹⁰4s¹.
  • The highest oxidation state in the 3d series is +7 for Mn. It rises up to Mn and then falls.
  • Colour comes from d–d transitions, so d⁰ and d¹⁰ ions such as Sc³⁺ and Zn²⁺ are colourless.
  • Lanthanoid contraction is a steady fall in size across the 4f series. It makes 4d and 5d elements of a group alike in size.
Memory hook

Mn is the multi-valent king.

Common trap

Counting Zn, Cd and Hg as transition elements in a 'why' question.

Chemistry7 marks

Coordination Compounds

Rules

  • Name ligands in alphabetical order, then the metal. Anionic complexes end in -ate. Show the oxidation state in Roman numerals.
  • [Ni(CN)₄]²⁻ is square planar (dsp², diamagnetic). [NiCl₄]²⁻ is tetrahedral (sp³, paramagnetic).
  • Strong-field ligands (CN⁻, CO, en, NH₃) pair electrons and give low spin. Halides and H₂O are weak field.
  • Isomerism to know: linkage, ionisation, hydrate, coordination, geometrical and optical.
Memory hook

C and N donors are strong. Halides are weak.

Common trap

Ordering ligands alphabetically by prefix, such as di- or tri-. Ignore prefixes.

Chemistry6 marks

Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

Rules

  • SN1 order is 3° > 2° > 1° (carbocation stability) with racemisation. SN2 order is 1° > 2° > 3° with inversion.
  • Leaving-group ability I⁻ > Br⁻ > Cl⁻ > F⁻, so reactivity is RI > RBr > RCl.
  • Finkelstein gives iodides (NaI in dry acetone). Swarts gives fluorides (AgF, Hg₂F₂, SbF₃).
  • Chlorobenzene resists nucleophilic substitution: resonance gives the C–Cl bond partial double-bond character, and the carbon is sp².
Memory hook

SN1 counts 3-2-1. SN2 counts 1-2-3.

Common trap

Treating a racemic product as a sign of SN2.

Chemistry6 marks

Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers

Rules

  • Lucas test (ZnCl₂/conc. HCl): 3° turns turbid at once, 2° in about 5 minutes and 1° does not at room temperature.
  • Phenol is more acidic than an alcohol because the phenoxide ion is stabilised by resonance. Electron-withdrawing groups raise acidity.
  • Dehydration with conc. H₂SO₄: 443 K gives an alkene and 413 K gives an ether.
  • Williamson synthesis: sodium alkoxide plus a primary alkyl halide.
Memory hook

Lower temperature, ether: two molecules join. Higher temperature, alkene.

Common trap

Using a tertiary halide in the Williamson synthesis. It eliminates.

Chemistry8 marks

Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids

Rules

  • Tollens' (silver mirror) and Fehling's (red precipitate, aliphatic aldehydes only) detect aldehydes. Iodoform detects CH₃CO– and CH₃CH(OH)–.
  • Rosenmund: acid chloride to aldehyde (H₂, Pd/BaSO₄). Stephen: nitrile to aldehyde (SnCl₂/HCl). Etard: toluene to benzaldehyde.
  • Aldol needs an α-hydrogen. Cannizzaro needs none (conc. NaOH).
  • Acid strength: carboxylic acid > phenol > alcohol. Electron-withdrawing groups raise acid strength.
Memory hook

Aldol Asks for alpha-H. Cannizzaro Cannot have it.

Common trap

Saying that benzaldehyde gives Fehling's test.

Chemistry6 marks

Amines

Rules

  • In water: methylamines 2° > 1° > 3° > NH₃. Ethylamines 2° > 3° > 1° > NH₃. Aromatic amines are weaker than aliphatic ones.
  • Hinsberg: the 1° product dissolves in alkali, the 2° product does not, and 3° does not react. Carbylamine test: only primary amines (foul smell).
  • Gabriel phthalimide gives pure 1° aliphatic amines. Hoffmann bromamide gives an amine with one carbon fewer.
  • Diazonium salts: Sandmeyer (CuCl, CuBr, CuCN), Gattermann (Cu powder) and coupling to azo dyes.
Memory hook

Methyl: 2-1-3. Ethyl: 2-3-1. Hoffmann hands over a carbon.

Common trap

Using the Gabriel synthesis for aromatic amines.

Chemistry7 marks

Biomolecules

Rules

  • Sucrose is non-reducing because both anomeric carbons are tied up in the glycosidic bond. Maltose and lactose are reducing.
  • Starch is amylose plus amylopectin (α-links). Cellulose has β-links, which humans cannot digest.
  • DNA pairs: A=T with two hydrogen bonds and G≡C with three. RNA has uracil and ribose.
  • Deficiency: vitamin A night blindness, B₁ beri-beri, C scurvy, D rickets, K delayed clotting.
Memory hook

A=T two, G≡C three.

Common trap

Stating that RNA contains thymine.

MathematicsUnit I · 8 marks

Relations and Functions

Rules

  • An equivalence relation is reflexive, symmetric and transitive. Check all three.
  • Bijective means one-one and onto. Only bijective functions have inverses.
  • From a set of m elements to a set of n elements there are nᵐ functions.
Memory hook

RST: Reflexive, Symmetric, Transitive.

Common trap

Checking symmetry with a single example instead of a general proof.

MathematicsUnit I · 8 marks

Inverse Trigonometric Functions

Rules

  • Principal values: sin⁻¹ ∈ [−π/2, π/2], tan⁻¹ ∈ (−π/2, π/2), cos⁻¹ ∈ [0, π], cot⁻¹ ∈ (0, π).
  • sin⁻¹x + cos⁻¹x = π/2 and tan⁻¹x + cot⁻¹x = π/2.
  • tan⁻¹x + tan⁻¹y = tan⁻¹((x + y)/(1 − xy)) only when xy < 1.
Memory hook

The sine family sits around zero. The cosine family sits from 0 to π.

Common trap

Dropping the condition xy < 1.

MathematicsUnit II · 10 marks

Matrices

Rules

  • (AB)ᵀ = BᵀAᵀ and (AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹. Reverse the order, like shoes and socks.
  • An m×n matrix times an n×p matrix is m×p. The inner numbers must match.
  • Every square matrix is a symmetric part plus a skew-symmetric part: A = ½(A + Aᵀ) + ½(A − Aᵀ).
Memory hook

Shoes and socks: reverse the order.

Common trap

Writing AB = BA.

MathematicsUnit II · 10 marks

Determinants

Rules

  • |kA| = kⁿ|A| for an n×n matrix. |adj A| = |A|ⁿ⁻¹.
  • A·adj A = |A|I. A⁻¹ exists only if |A| ≠ 0.
  • For AX = B: |A| ≠ 0 gives a unique solution. |A| = 0 with (adj A)B = 0 gives infinitely many solutions, and (adj A)B ≠ 0 gives none.
  • Area of a triangle = ½ times the absolute value of the coordinate determinant.
Memory hook

Zero determinant: ask what (adj A)B is.

Common trap

Forgetting the cofactor sign pattern + − +.

MathematicsUnit III · 35 marks

Continuity and Differentiability

Rules

  • Continuity at a: LHL = RHL = f(a). Differentiable implies continuous, not the other way round.
  • For x^x, (sin x)^x and similar forms, take logarithms first.
  • For inverse trigonometric derivatives, substitute x = tanθ or sinθ to simplify before differentiating.
  • Chain rule: differentiate the outside, then multiply by the derivative of the inside.
Memory hook

Differentiable ⇒ continuous. Not the reverse (|x| at 0).

Common trap

Skipping the derivative of the inside function.

MathematicsUnit III · 35 marks

Applications of Derivatives

Rules

  • Rates: dV/dt = (dV/dr)(dr/dt). Write down what is changing before differentiating.
  • Tangent slope m = dy/dx. Normal slope = −1/m.
  • Maxima and minima: write the quantity as a function of one variable, set the derivative to zero, then test with the second derivative.
  • f′(x) > 0 means increasing and f′(x) < 0 means decreasing.
Memory hook

Express, differentiate, zero, test, answer with units.

Common trap

Declaring a maximum without checking the second derivative.

MathematicsUnit III · 35 marks

Integrals

Rules

  • For integration by parts choose u by ILATE: Inverse, Logarithmic, Algebraic, Trigonometric, Exponential.
  • ∫eˣ[f(x) + f′(x)]dx = eˣf(x) + C.
  • ∫₀ᵃ f(x)dx = ∫₀ᵃ f(a − x)dx. An even function on [−a, a] gives 2∫₀ᵃ and an odd function gives 0.
  • Factor the denominator first. Partial fractions split distinct linear factors as A/(x − a) + B/(x − b).
Memory hook

ILATE picks u.

Common trap

Leaving out + C on an indefinite integral.

MathematicsUnit III · 35 marks

Applications of Integrals

Rules

  • Sketch, shade, find the intersection points, then choose between a vertical and a horizontal strip.
  • Area between curves = ∫(upper − lower) dx.
  • A circle x² + y² = a² has area πa². An ellipse has area πab.
Memory hook

Sketch first, integrate second.

Common trap

Taking the lower curve minus the upper curve and getting a negative area.

MathematicsUnit III · 35 marks

Differential Equations

Rules

  • Order is the highest derivative. Degree is its power when the equation is a polynomial in derivatives.
  • Variable separable: put all y terms with dy and all x terms with dx.
  • Homogeneous: put y = vx. Linear: dy/dx + Py = Q, with integrating factor e^(∫P dx).
  • A general solution has as many arbitrary constants as the order.
Memory hook

Linear form: P and Q depend only on x.

Common trap

Leaving the integrating factor off the right-hand side.

MathematicsUnit IV · 14 marks

Vectors

Rules

  • The dot product is a scalar and uses cosθ. The cross product is a vector and uses sinθ.
  • Projection of a on b = a·b/|b|. Parallelogram area = |a × b|. Triangle area = ½|a × b|.
  • The scalar triple product [a b c] is the volume of a parallelepiped. Zero means coplanar.
Memory hook

Dot gives Cosine. Cross gives Sine.

Common trap

Writing a × b = b × a. The sign reverses.

MathematicsUnit IV · 14 marks

Three Dimensional Geometry

Rules

  • The angle between two lines, or two planes, uses cosine of the angle between direction vectors, or normals.
  • The angle between a line and a plane uses sine: sinθ = |b·n|/(|b||n|).
  • Distance from a point to a plane: |ax₁ + by₁ + cz₁ + d|/√(a² + b² + c²).
  • Shortest distance between skew lines: |(a₂ − a₁)·(b₁ × b₂)|/|b₁ × b₂|. For parallel lines use |(a₂ − a₁) × b|/|b|.
Memory hook

Line and plane: use Sine.

Common trap

Reading direction ratios from an equation that is not in standard form.

MathematicsUnit V · 5 marks

Linear Programming

Rules

  • Steps: define the variables, write the objective and constraints, draw the lines, shade the feasible region, list the corner points and evaluate Z at each.
  • For a bounded region the optimum is at a corner point.
  • For an unbounded region, test the value against the open half-plane given by the objective before accepting it (the NCERT method).
Memory hook

Corners decide.

Common trap

Shading the wrong side of a constraint.

MathematicsUnit VI · 8 marks

Probability

Rules

  • Conditional: P(A|B) = P(A∩B)/P(B). Independent: P(A∩B) = P(A)P(B).
  • Bayes: draw the tree. P(Eᵢ|A) = P(Eᵢ)P(A|Eᵢ) / ΣP(Eⱼ)P(A|Eⱼ). The denominator is the total probability of A.
  • Binomial: P(X = k) = ⁿCₖ pᵏ qⁿ⁻ᵏ, with mean np and variance npq.
  • Variance of a distribution = E(X²) − [E(X)]².
Memory hook

Bayes: Tree, then Total, then Ratio.

Common trap

Putting the probability of the cause, not of the effect, in the denominator.

Memory methods

How to make the rules stay.

Reading again feels productive and fades fast. These methods take recall from the page to the head.

1

Blank-page recall

Close the book. Write everything you remember about a chapter on a blank page, then add what you missed in another colour.

2

Formula, unit, condition

Store each formula as a triple: the formula, its unit and when it applies. "ΔT_f = iK_f m: K_f in K kg mol⁻¹, m in mol per kg, dilute solution".

3

Spaced revision

Revisit new material after 1 day, 3 days, 7 days and 21 days. Each pass can be 10 minutes. A common study pattern, not a CBSE rule.

4

Teach it in two minutes

Explain a derivation or a reaction to a friend or to a wall. Where you stumble is where to revise.

5

Derivation tick-list

List the derivations and name reactions in each subject. Tick one only after writing it from scratch and comparing it with NCERT.

6

Error log

Write each mistake with its cause: concept, calculation, units, reading or time. Read the log before every test.
